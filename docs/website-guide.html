Start here — your first ten minutes

If you only do five things today, do these.

  1. Open your website address followed by /admin.php and sign in.
  2. Go straight to Settings → Admin Account and change the default password.
  3. Check Settings → Company & Contact Info — phone numbers and address show on every page of the public site.
  4. Open Inquiries and see whether any client is waiting for a reply.
  5. Add one product from start to finish using section 04, then open the public website to see it live.
01

How the site fits together

Your website has two halves. Customers only ever see the first one; you work in the second.

The halfAddressWho sees itWhat it is
The public website ethiotractors.com Everyone Your homepage, industries, product catalog, brands and contact form.
The admin panel ethiotractors.com/admin.php Staff only, after signing in Where you add machines, read inquiries and edit company details.

There is no separate "publish" or "go live" button anywhere in the panel. The moment you save something in the admin panel, it is live on the public website. Refresh the public page and the change is already there.

What you can change yourself

Everything in this list is yours to edit, any time, with no developer involved:

  • Products — add, edit, photograph, reorder and delete them
  • The order machines appear in on the catalog page
  • Company name, phone numbers, email, address, branches and working hours
  • The map location on the contact page
  • Your Telegram, Facebook and LinkedIn links
  • Trade licence number
  • Your own username and password

What needs your developer

  • Page layout, colours, fonts and the homepage hero images
  • The wording of the About Us section and the industry descriptions
  • Adding brand logos to the Brands strip
  • New pages or new sections
Good to know

The admin panel is deliberately hidden from Google — it is marked "do not index", so it will never turn up in search results. It is protected by your username and password alone, which is why section 02 asks you to change that password before anything else.

02

Signing in

Type your website address in the browser, add /admin.php to the end, and press Enter. You will land on the staff portal.

  1. Type your username. On a brand-new site this is admin.
  2. Type your password. On a brand-new site this is ethio2026.
  3. Press Sign In. You arrive at the Dashboard.
Do this first

While the site is still using the default password, the login page prints those default details on screen for anyone who visits it. That hint disappears automatically the moment you set your own password — which is exactly why changing it is the first job. Jump to section 08 and change it now, then come back.

Three things the login page does to protect you

BehaviourWhat you will seeWhat to do
Automatic sign-out
after 1 hour with no activity		 "You were signed out after an hour of inactivity." Simply sign in again. Nothing you saved is lost.
Wrong-password lockout
after 6 failed tries in 10 minutes		 "Too many failed attempts — please wait a few minutes and try again." Wait roughly ten minutes, then try once more, carefully.
Expired session
on a form left open too long		 "Session expired — please try again." Sign in again and re-enter the form. This protects you from forged requests.
Tip

Bookmark /admin.php in your browser so you never have to type it. Sign out using the Sign Out button at the bottom of the left menu whenever you finish on a shared or public computer.

04

Adding a product

This is the job you will do most often, so it is worth doing once slowly. We will add a real machine — a Doğanlar heavy disc harrow — from the empty form to a live card on the public website.

  1. Open the form

    Click Products in the left menu, then the red + Add Product button at the top right. An empty form opens with two cards: Product Details on the left, Card Artwork on the right.

  2. Fill in the product details

    1. Product Name — required. What the customer reads first, e.g. Doganlar Heavy Disc Harrow HDH-32.
    2. Sector — required. Decides which tab of the public catalog the machine appears under.
    3. Category — the small grey line under the name, e.g. Harrows & Tillage.
    4. Brand — e.g. Doganlar, Zoomlion, Romsan. Printed above the product name on the card.
    5. Description — one or two sentences. This is the paragraph on the product card.
    6. Tags — separate with commas. Only the first two are shown on the card.
    7. + Add specification — builds the mini spec sheet. Covered in the next step.

    Field limits at a glance

    FieldRequiredMaximum lengthWhere it shows
    Product NameYes160 charactersCard heading
    SectorYesPick one of fourCatalog tab
    CategoryNo80 charactersSmall line above the name
    BrandNo80 charactersSmall line above the name
    DescriptionNo600 charactersParagraph on the card
    TagsNo200 charactersGrey chips, first two only
    Watch out

    If you leave the name empty or forget the sector, the panel refuses to save and tells you "A product needs at least a name and a sector." Everything else is optional.

    The four sectors are fixed: Agriculture, Construction, Mining and Power & Logistics. Choose the one a customer would look under, not the one the factory uses.

  3. Add the specifications

    Specifications become the small grey table on the product card — the numbers a buyer scans before they call you. Click + Add specification once per line.

    1. Label — the left column, e.g. Working width. Up to 60 characters.
    2. Value — the right column, e.g. 3.2 m. Up to 100 characters.
    3. × removes that row. There is no confirmation, but nothing is saved until you press the save button.
    4. + Add specification adds another empty row underneath.
    Good to know

    A row is only saved when both the label and the value are filled in. Half-empty rows are quietly dropped, so you can leave a spare blank row on screen without it appearing on the website.

    Tip

    Four to six specifications reads best. Pick the ones buyers actually compare — working width, power requirement, capacity, weight — and keep the same labels across similar machines so customers can compare them at a glance.

  4. Add the photo

    Move to the Card Artwork card on the right.

    1. Preview. Your photo appears here immediately — before saving. If it looks wrong here, it will look wrong on the site.
    2. Choose File. Picks a photo from your computer or phone.
    3. Image link. An alternative — paste a web address of a photo instead of uploading one.
    4. Fallback icon. A simple line drawing used only when there is no photo at all. Pick the closest machine type.
    5. Add To Catalog. Saves everything on both cards. Nothing is stored until you press this.

    Photo rules

    RuleDetail
    Accepted formats.jpg   .png   .webp   .gif
    Maximum size5 MB per photo
    If you do bothAn uploaded file always wins over a pasted link.
    If you do neitherThe fallback line-art icon is shown instead.
    Watch out

    If the file is too big, or is not really an image, the panel refuses it with "Photo not saved — please use a JPG, PNG, WebP or GIF image under 5 MB." Renaming a file to .jpg does not make it one — the panel checks the actual contents.

    Tip — photos that sell
    • Shoot the whole machine, side-on, with nothing cut off at the edges.
    • A plain background — a clear yard or a wall — beats a busy workshop.
    • Keep all your photos roughly the same shape, so the catalog rows line up neatly.
    • Photos straight from a phone are often 8–12 MB. If yours is refused, reduce it before uploading.

  5. Check the whole form before saving

    This is the completed form, both cards filled in. Read it once from top to bottom — it is far easier to fix a typo now than after a customer has seen it.

  6. Save it

    Press Add To Catalog →. The panel returns you to the product list with a green confirmation across the top.

    New products are added to the end of the catalog. To move the machine further up, see section 05.

See it on the public website

Click View Website ↗ at the bottom of the left menu, go to the Products section and find your machine. Everything you typed is now working for you:

Read the card against what you typed: the brand and category sit above the name, the description is the paragraph, the four specifications became the grey table, the first two tags became chips, and Get A Quote → sends the customer to your contact form with this machine already filled in as their interest.

That is the whole loop

Fill in a form, press save, and the machine is selling for you. Every product on your site was added exactly this way.

05

Editing, ordering and deleting

The product list is the control desk for your whole catalog.

  1. Sector tabs. Narrow the list to one sector, or All.
  2. Filter by name. Type to find a machine instantly.
  3. Drag hint. Reminds you the ⠿ handle sets the order on the website.
  4. Edit and Delete for that row.
  5. + Add Product — the form from section 04.

Finding one machine in a long catalog

Two tools, and they work together. Typing in the filter box hides every row that does not match — the name, the category and the brand are all searched:

Clicking a sector tab does the same thing by sector:

Editing a product

Press Edit on any row. The same form from section 04 opens, already filled in. Change what you need and press Save Changes →.

  1. Remove the current photo — tick this to go back to the line-art icon. It only takes effect when you save.
  2. Save Changes → — updates the public website immediately.
Good to know

To swap a photo, just choose a new file — you do not need to remove the old one first. The panel replaces it and tidies the old file off the server for you.

Changing the order machines appear in

The order of the rows in this list is exactly the order customers see on the public catalog. Put your best sellers at the top.

  1. The hint bar explains both ways to reorder, and reports back when the new order has been saved.
  2. The ⠿ handle. Hold it and drag the row up or down. On a tablet, press and drag with your finger.

Prefer the keyboard? Click a ⠿ handle once to select it, then press ↑ or ↓ to walk the row up and down the list.

There is no save button — the new order saves itself a moment after you let go, and the hint bar confirms with "Order saved".

Watch out

You cannot reorder while the filter box has text in it — rows are hidden rather than removed, so a drop between two of them would be a guess. The bar will say "Clear the filter to reorder products." Empty the filter box and the handles work again.

Good to know

Reordering inside a single sector tab only shuffles that sector. The rest of the catalog stays exactly where it was.

Deleting a product

Press Delete on the row. You are always asked to confirm first:

Deleting cannot be undone

There is no rubbish bin and no undo. The product and its photo are gone, and it disappears from the public catalog at once. If a machine is only out of stock, edit the description instead of deleting it — you keep the photo, the specifications and its position in the list.

06

Client inquiries

Every contact form on the public website lands here. This is your sales inbox — the number in the red badge is money waiting for a phone call.

  1. Status tabs with a live count of each.
  2. Click any row to open the full message underneath it.
  3. Source — where it came from: contact is the full contact form, newsletter is an email-only signup.
  4. Status — how far along you are.
  5. Export CSV — downloads every inquiry as a spreadsheet.

Reading and answering one

Click the row. It expands in place, showing the message and all the contact details:

  1. The message exactly as the client typed it.
  2. Their details — email, phone, company, industry and the machine they asked about.
  3. Reply By Email opens your email program with the address and subject line already written.
  4. Mark Contacted once you have replied or called. Mark Closed when the job is finished.
  5. Delete removes the inquiry permanently.

On a phone, Call Client dials the number directly.

The three statuses

StatusMeansSet it when
new Nobody has dealt with this yet. Counted in the red badge. Set automatically when the client submits the form.
contacted You have replied or called. The conversation is live. Straight after you reply — it clears the badge for your colleagues.
closed Finished, whether it became a sale or not. When there is nothing further to do.
A simple daily routine
  1. Open Inquiries and click the New tab.
  2. Work down the list, oldest first — the newest are at the top, so start at the bottom.
  3. Reply or call, then immediately press Mark Contacted.
  4. Aim to leave the tab empty. An empty New tab means nobody is waiting.

Exporting to a spreadsheet

⬇ Export CSV downloads every inquiry — all statuses, all sources — as a file named like ethiotractors-inquiries-2026-08-04.csv. It opens directly in Excel with the Amharic and accented characters intact. Useful for a monthly sales review or for importing into another system.

Good to know about times

On screen, every date and time is shown in Addis Ababa time. Inside the exported spreadsheet the column is labelled Date (UTC) and runs three hours behind — that is normal and is there so the file is unambiguous wherever it is opened.

Newsletter signups

Rows marked newsletter are people who only left an email address on the public site. They have no message, no phone number and no company, so the expanded view is mostly empty — that is expected. Keep them; they are your mailing list.

07

Site reports

Site Reports answers a commercial question: which machines are people actually interested in?

  1. The headline figures for the last 30 days.
  2. Daily activity for the last 14 days — visits, quote clicks and inquiries side by side.
  3. Most Quoted Products — which machines customers pressed Get A Quote on.
  4. Inquiry Pipeline — your whole inbox at a glance, by status and by source.

What each figure means

FigureMeaningHow to read it
Page ViewsPages opened in the last 30 days.One person browsing four pages counts four times.
Unique VisitorsSeparate people, last 30 days.The honest measure of reach.
Quote ClicksTaps on Get A Quote.Real buying intent, machine by machine.
ConversionInquiries ÷ visitors, as a percentage.How well the site turns interest into contact.
Put it to work

Most Quoted Products is the most useful table on this page. A machine near the top with few actual inquiries usually means the card is not answering a buyer's question — try a better photo, clearer specifications, or a description that names the work the machine does. Machines that quote well deserve to be dragged to the top of the catalog.

Privacy

Visitor counting is anonymous. No names, no IP addresses and no personal details are stored — only an unnamed browser key used to avoid counting the same person twice. Figures start from the day the feature went live, so an empty chart on a new site simply means no visits have been recorded yet.

08

Company settings

Settings holds three separate boxes: your company details, your login, and a danger zone. Each has its own save button — pressing one does not save the others.

Company & contact information

These fields feed the top bar, the contact section, the footer and the map on every page of the public site. Change a phone number here and it changes everywhere at once.

  1. Company Name — used across the site and in the subject line of email replies.
  2. Phone — the main number. Two more optional numbers sit beside it.
  3. Head Office Address — printed in the contact section and the footer.
  4. Map Location — a place name or address that positions the Google map. See the warning below.
  5. Telegram, Facebook, LinkedIn — paste the full web address, starting with https://. Leave blank to hide the icon.
Watch out — the map is a separate field

Map Location is not the same as Head Office Address. Changing your address does not move the map. Update both, and use a form Google Maps recognises for the map field — for example Bole, Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. After saving, open the public contact page and check the pin landed in the right place.

Press Save Settings → and the confirmation reads "Site settings saved — the public website is updated." Every field accepts up to 400 characters.

Your admin account

This is where you change the default password — the job from section 02.

  1. Username — change it if you want something other than admin.
  2. New Password — at least 8 characters. Leave it empty to keep your current one.
  3. Current Password — always required, even when you are only changing the username.

Press Update Account →. If the current password is wrong, nothing at all is changed and you are told so.

Choosing a good password
  • Eight characters is the minimum the panel accepts — twelve or more is genuinely safer.
  • Three unrelated words with a number are easy to remember and hard to guess.
  • Do not reuse the password from your email account.
  • Everyone shares this one login, so tell the rest of the team when you change it — and change it again whenever someone leaves.

The danger zone

ButtonWhat it really doesWhen to use it
Restore Default Catalog Wipes every product — including all of yours, with their photos — and puts back the original import list. Almost never. Only to start the catalog again from nothing.
Delete Closed Inquiries Permanently removes every inquiry marked closed. New and contacted ones are untouched. Occasional tidying. Export the CSV first if you want the history.
Neither can be undone

Restore Default Catalog destroys weeks of cataloguing in one click. Both buttons ask you to confirm, and that confirmation is the only thing standing between you and the loss — read it before pressing Yes, do it.

09

On a phone or tablet

The whole admin panel works on a phone. Handy for answering a client from the yard, or photographing a machine and adding it where it stands.

The left menu is hidden to save room. Tap the ☰ button in the corner to slide it in, and tap anywhere outside it to slide it away.

Adding a machine from your phone

Photograph the machine, open Products → + Add Product, and at Choose File your phone offers its camera and photo library. Do check the size — phone photos are often over the 5 MB limit and will be refused.

10

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhyWhat to do
"Invalid username or password." A typo, or the password has been changed by a colleague. Check caps lock. After six failed tries you are locked out for a few minutes.
"Too many failed attempts." Six wrong passwords within ten minutes. Wait ten minutes and try again, carefully.
"You were signed out after an hour of inactivity." Normal security. The panel logs you out when idle. Sign in again. Saved work is safe.
"Session expired — please try again." A form sat open too long before being submitted. Sign in again and re-enter the form.
"A product needs at least a name and a sector." One of the two required fields is empty. Fill in the product name and choose a sector.
"Photo not saved…" Over 5 MB, or not a real image file. Reduce the photo, or re-save it as a JPG or PNG.
"Clear the filter to reorder products." Rows are hidden by the filter box, so dragging is ambiguous. Empty the filter box, then drag.
"Could not save the order…" The connection dropped, or someone else edited the catalog at the same moment. Reload the page and set the order again.
"Your current password is incorrect." The confirmation password in the Account box is wrong. Nothing was changed. Retype it and save again.
A change is not showing on the public site. Your browser is showing you a stored copy of the page. Reload the public page. On a computer, hold Shift while pressing reload.
The site says it is temporarily unavailable. The website cannot reach its database. This is a server problem, not something you did. Contact your developer or hosting provider — nothing in the admin panel can fix it.

  • Add a product, edit one, or change the catalog order

Stop and think first

  • Deleting a product — the photo and specifications go with it
  • Deleting an inquiry — you lose the client's details and their message

The one habit that matters

After any change, click View Website ↗ and look at the public page. It takes five seconds, and it is the only way to be certain your customers see what you intended.
